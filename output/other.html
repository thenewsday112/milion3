Brownie Batter Bark

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1-2/3 cups almond flour
  • 1/3 cup baking cocoa
  • 3 tablespoons packed brown sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup coconut oil, melted and slightly cooled
  • 1-1/2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 1-1/2 cups miniature semisweet chocolate chips, divided
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. Line a 15x10x1-in. baking sheet with parchment paper.
  2. In a large bowl, combine almond flour, cocoa powder, brown sugar and salt. Stir in melted coconut oil and vanilla until smooth. Spread onto the prepared baking sheet in an even layer. Freeze 20 minutes.
  3. Meanwhile, in a small microwave-safe bowl, microwave 1 cup chocolate chips in 30-second increments until melted, stirring in between. Spread over the frozen bark. Sprinkle with the remaining 1/2 cup chocolate chips.
  4. Freeze until firm, about 1 hour. Break into bark pieces.

Nutrition Facts

1 serving: 588 calories, 45g fat (25g saturated fat), 0 cholesterol, 203mg sodium, 44g carbohydrate (33g sugars, 8g fiber), 9g protein.

Copycat Taco Bell Caramel Apple Empanadas

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1-1/2 cups finely chopped peeled tart apples
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/8 teaspoon ground allspice
  • 1/3 cup thick caramel apple dip
  • 2 sheets refrigerated pie crust
  • 1 large egg yolk
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 1/4 cup butter, melted
  • 1 cup cinnamon sugar
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. In a small saucepan, combine apples, sugar, cinnamon and allspice. Cook until apples are tender, 4-5 minutes. Drain; discard liquid. Transfer to a small bowl. Add caramel; stir to coat. Scoop by tablespoonfuls onto waxed paper. Freeze until firm, about 1 hour.
  2. Unroll pie crust; cut into 14 circles using a 4-in. circle cookie cutter (rerolling dough scraps as needed).
  3. In a small bowl, whisk together egg yolk and water. Brush edges of crusts with egg wash; place apple filling in the center. Fold crusts over filling and seal edges well with a fork. Place 2 in. apart on parchment-lined baking sheets. Freeze until firm, about 30 minutes.
  4. Brush remaining egg wash over tops. Cut 2 small slits on the top of each empanada.
  5. Bake at 425° for 12-14 minutes or until golden brown. Remove from oven and brush with melted butter. Carefully toss in cinnamon sugar. Serve warm.

Nutrition Facts

1 piece: 234 calories, 13g fat (6g saturated fat), 29mg cholesterol, 159mg sodium, 28g carbohydrate (14g sugars, 0 fiber), 2g protein.

Cherry Peach Pie with Salted Pecan Streusel

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1-1/3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1-1/2 teaspoons sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon pumpkin pie spice
  • 4 tablespoons shortening, cubed
  • 4 tablespoons cold butter, cubed
  • 3 to 4 tablespoons water
  • TOPPING:
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1/4 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/4 cup crushed gingersnap cookies
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup old-fashioned oats
  • 1/2 cup chopped pecans
  • 1/4 cup butter, melted
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • FILLING:
  • 2 cups sliced peeled peaches
  • 1 cup frozen pitted dark sweet cherries, thawed
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/3 cup sugar
  • 1/3 cup packed brown sugar
  • 4 tablespoons potato starch
  • Pinch salt
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. In a large bowl, mix flour, sugar, salt and pie spice; cut in shortening and butter until crumbly. Gradually add ice water, tossing with a fork until dough holds together when pressed. Shape into a disk; wrap and refrigerate 1 hour or overnight.
  2. Preheat oven to 400°. On a lightly floured surface, roll dough to a 1/8-in.-thick circle; transfer to a 9-in. pie plate. Trim crust to 1/2 in. beyond rim of plate; flute edge. Refrigerate while preparing filling.
  3. For the topping, in a medium bowl, whisk together flour, sugars, crushed cookies and salt. Stir in oats and pecans. Add the butter and vanilla until small clumps form. Set aside.
  4. In a large bowl, toss peaches and cherries with lemon juice and vanilla. In a separate bowl, whisk together sugars, potato starch and salt. Add to fruit, toss until well coated.
  5. Transfer filling to prepared crust. Sprinkle with reserved topping; pressing down to adhere as needed. Bake for 20 minutes. Reduce oven setting to 350°, bake until crust and topping are golden brown and fruit is bubbly, 20-25 minutes longer. Cool completely on a wire rack.

Nutrition Facts

1 piece: 503 calories, 23g fat (9g saturated fat), 31mg cholesterol, 563mg sodium, 70g carbohydrate (38g sugars, 2g fiber), 5g protein.

French Onion Stuffing

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 16 ounces sourdough bread, cut into 1-in. cubes
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 3 pounds medium onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon pepper
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/4 cup dry sherry
  • 2 tablespoons minced fresh thyme sprigs, plus more for garnish
  • 2 large eggs, room temperature
  • 2 cups reduced-sodium beef broth
  • 6 ounces Gruyere cheese, grated
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. Preheat oven to 350°. Spread bread cubes in a single layer on two baking sheets. Bake 10 minutes; flip bread cubes and rotate baking sheets. Bake until very dry and golden brown, 5-8 minutes. Set aside to cool.
  2. Meanwhile, in a Dutch oven, heat butter and olive oil over medium heat. Add onions; cook until softened, 10-15 minutes, stirring frequently. Stir in salt and pepper. Reduce heat to medium-low; cook until deep golden brown, 40-50 minutes, stirring occasionally. Add garlic; cook until fragrant, 30-60 seconds. Add dry sherry; use a wooden spoon to scrape up browned bits. Stir in thyme. Remove from heat.
  3. In a large bowl, whisk eggs until smooth. Stir in cooked onions and bread cubes. Stir in beef broth until evenly moistened.
  4. Spoon stuffing into a greased 13x9-in. or 3 quart baking dish. Cover with foil; bake 30 minutes. Uncover; bake until crispy and browned, 15-20 minutes.
  5. Remove from the oven; increase oven temperature to 425°. Sprinkle cheese on top. Bake until melted and bubbly, 3-5 minutes. Garnish with thyme to serve.

Nutrition Facts

1 serving: 394 calories, 16g fat (7g saturated fat), 79mg cholesterol, 915mg sodium, 46g carbohydrate (10g sugars, 4g fiber), 16g protein.

Carne Guisada

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1 boneless beef chuck roast (3 pounds), cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt, divided
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon paprika
  • 2 tablespoons canola oil
  • 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1 jalapeno pepper, seeded and chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 bottle (12 ounces) beer
  • 2 cups beef broth
  • 4 teaspoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 2 to 3 teaspoons crushed red pepper flakes
  • 2 teaspoons chili powder
  • 1-1/2 teaspoons ground cumin
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 2 large unpeeled red potatoes, chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon red wine vinegar
  • Tortillas, hot cooked rice, lime wedges and chopped fresh cilantro, optional
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. In a large bowl, toss beef and 1/4 teaspoon salt. In a separate large bowl, combine flour and paprika. Add beef, a few pieces at a time, and toss to coat.
  2. In a Dutch oven, heat oil over medium heat. Brown beef in batches, adding additional oil as necessary. Remove with a slotted spoon. In same pan, add onion; cook and stir until tender, 3-5 minutes. Stir in tomato paste, jalapeno and garlic; cook until fragrant and color starts to darken slightly. Add browned beef back to the pan. Add beer; bring to a boil, scraping the bottom to release browned bits. Simmer until reduced by half, about 5 minutes. Stir in broth, Worcestershire sauce, crushed red pepper flakes, chili powder, cumin, bay leaf and remaining 1/2 teaspoon salt; bring to a boil. Every year during Thanksgiving, I feel compelled to make a turkey dinner because I love a good tradition. But as a Thai, I can't help but put some Thai twists on it for a little bolder and more complex flavours. In this recipe, turkey breast and thigh are marinated in a classic Thai meat marinade, then roasted on a bed of lemongrass and other Thai herbs. The herb-infused drippings get turned into our coconut gravy. It is sublime. If you still want a traditional vibe to your turkey dinner, but are getting tired of the same old, this recipe is for you! It can also be made gluten free.

Note: Over the years I have decided that my favourite way to roast turkey is to roast breast and thighs instead of roasting a whole turkey. This yields the juiciest, most flavourful meat, a short cooking time, easiest carving, and you can scale it up or down to suit the size of your party. And I think the serving is still just as beautiful! If you still want to roast a whole turkey, I will provide some guidance.

TL;DR - Recipe Overview

Here's a quick snapshot of what's involved, so you can plan accordingly!

The turkey: Turkey breasts and/or thighs are first marinated; I will explain why I prefer roasting parts rather than a whole turkey. They're roasted on a bed of lemongrass, galangal, lime leaves, and shallots.

The gravy: The marinade and the herb-infused drippings are added into the gravy, which is then enriched with coconut milk.

How long it takes: Give the meat at least 3 hours to marinate and up to overnight. Roasting takes only about 45 minutes, plus 15-20 mins resting time. The gravy can be started while the turkey is roasting, and finished during resting.

What it can be served with: This will still work perfectly with your traditional sides - mashed potatoes, stuffing, salads, roasted veg, etc. I will provide some Thai side ideas below.

Optional flair: To make it a little more Thai, serve with the classic Thai dipping sauce for meat, nam jim jaew.

Why You Should Roast Turkey Parts Instead of a Whole Bird

I know there’s nothing more impressive than a whole roasted turkey in the middle of the Thanksgiving table, but let me try to convince you that if you prioritize flavour, juiciness AND practicality, you should roast turkey parts instead.

The Problem with Roasting a Whole Turkey

  • It’s nearly impossible to cook evenly. The bird is so big that the outside usually overcooks before the inside is done, or the breasts are dry by the time the thighs are cooked through. You can try to keep it juicy by brining or dry-brining, but that adds a long, cumbersome step and requires a lot of fridge space.
  • It’s hard to marinate. This recipe is so flavourful because of the Thai marinade. To marinate a whole bird, you’d need a giant bucket of marinade, which is impractical and wasteful.
  • It’s pretty stressful! Cooking a whole turkey is basically a whole-afternoon ordeal, and we all know the nervous moment at the end when you’re not sure whether you’re about to cut into a bird that’s raw inside or completely dry. Turkey parts take a fraction of the time and are so much easier to temp properly. Most importantly, you can take the breast and thigh out at different times, so each part cooks to its optimal doneness!

Roasting turkey parts solves all of these issues. And while you don’t get the classic whole-bird presentation, I think turkey breast and thigh on a platter of lemongrass is just as beautiful and impressive!

Can I Use This Recipe for a Spatchcocked Turkey?

If you REALLY want a whole bird, a good compromise is to spatchcock your turkey (or, ideally, have a butcher do it), which helps it cook more evenly. Marinating will still be a challenge, but it’s more doable than with a round bird.

If you have experience spatchcocking, you can use this recipe’s marinade, the herb bed, and the gravy, but you’ll need to consult another trusted recipe, created specifically for spatchcocked turkey, for the cooking time and temperature.

Keep in mind that this marinade contains sugar, so the turkey will brown faster than usual. If you’re extending the cooking time, you’ll likely need to lower the temperature.

Important Ingredient Notes

Here are all the important notes on ingredients, including substitutions. Amounts are in the full recipe card below!

Turkey

  • Turkey breast and/or thigh. I chose to use one boneless breast and one bone-in thigh so I have both dark and white meat to choose from, just like a whole turkey! If you have a strong preference, you can go with 2 breasts or 4 thighs. Roasting in parts also allows you to scale up according to the number of people, so you can do 2 breasts and 2 thighs if you've got a larger party. You can even add 2 drumsticks and you'll basically have a whole turkey, but much easier to roast!
  • Lemongrass. You want whole stalks of lemongrass as these will form the "bed" for your turkey to roast on. Frozen whole lemongrass works fine for this.
  • Makrut lime leaves. These will add a beautiful, citrusy aroma to the gravy which will help make the meal feel fresher. Fresh or frozen work well.
  • Galangal. Optional, but adds lovely flavour if you can find it! Frozen is fine.
  • Ginger. I don't normally add ginger to the "Thai trinity" that is the 3 previous herbs, but since this isn't meant to be traditional, I think it works quite well!
  • Shallots. Red onions or regular onions can also work. These will soften and naturally sweeten the drippings.

Marinade

  • White and black peppercorns. I like to use both black and white as they both have different flavours, but you can choose one or the other.
  • Garlic
  • Cilantro stems, or cilantro roots. Traditionally we would use cilantro roots for marinades as they are very aromatic and hold up well in pastes. But cilantro stems work great as a substitute.
  • Fish sauce. Use good quality fish sauce such as Squid, Megachef or Red Boat. You can read more about how to choose good fish sauce here.
  • Soy sauce. I use Thai soy sauce, but any kind of regular or light soy sauce you have will work. To make this gluten free, use gluten free soy sauce.
  • Black or dark soy sauce. This is added for colour, so the turkey will darken beautifully. You do not have to add it. If making gluten free, you can omit, or use GF dark soy sauce (though I have not seen that yet!)
  • Palm sugar. The sweetness will balance the saltiness of the soy sauce and fish sauce. You can sub an equal amount of brown sugar.

Gravy

  • Chicken stock, or turkey stock, of course! The most important thing is to make sure your stock is unsalted or low sodium, as we will be adding soy sauce and fish sauce into it. Store-bought is fine, but if you wanna DIY here's my recipe for an Asian style chicken stock.
  • Coconut milk. This will add a little creaminess and coconut aroma to our gravy. My favourite brand is Aroy-D in UHT paper carton. It is leaps and bounds better than canned coconut milk!! Read more about that in my Ultimate Guide to Coconut Milk.
  • Cornstarch. Gravy thickener. You can use all-purpose flour instead if you wish.
  • Lime juice or lemon juice (optional). You may or may not need this, but I find that if the gravy tastes a little flat or rich, a little squeeze of lime or lemon can sharpen the flavours and make it so much more palatable. You can decide whether to add it at the end when you are doing final taste adjustment for the gravy.
  • Black or dark soy sauce (optional). If you want your gravy a little darker, which always makes it look more delicious, an extra glug of dark or black soy sauce does the trick!

The Easy Way to Make a Creamy Chicken Lasagna

Some nights, boiling pasta and tossing it with a jar of sauce is all I have the energy for. But when I’m craving something a little more special without spending ages layering a lasagna, this is my happy middle ground. And my favorite budget-friendly part? I only need 1 lb. of chicken breast (usually just 1-2 breasts!) to make six hearty servings, thanks to the noodles and broccoli bulking everything out. I season and sear the chicken first, then sauté the onion and garlic in the same skillet. The broken lasagna noodles cook right in the chicken broth, soaking up all the flavor from the garlic and Italian seasoning as they soften. It’s a similar idea to our skillet lasagna, but with a creamy chicken and broccoli twist!

To keep this a true one-pot meal, I don’t cook the broccoli separately, either. I chop it finely, stems and all (because why waste them?!), and add it towards the end so it stays tender and bright green. Then a little heavy cream and Parmesan bring everything together in a rich, comforting sauce, and the mozzarella on top gives you a classic cheesy finish. Recipe Success Tips

  1. Break the noodles into roughly 2-inch pieces. I find this size works well for stirring the lasagna noodles through the skillet without them getting tangled up. It makes scooping out individual servings much easier, too.
  2. Give everything enough room to cook. You’ll need a large, deep, broiler-safe skillet with a lid, about 12 inches in diameter. I recommend a heavy-bottomed skillet because it heats more evenly, helping the noodles cook at the same rate without sticking to the bottom (see the skillet I use in the photo above).
  3. Keep the lid on during simmering. Press the broken noodles down so they’re mostly submerged in the broth, then cover and maintain a gentle simmer. I give them an occasional stir to separate any pieces sticking together, then pop the lid straight back on. The trapped steam helps cook the noodles that aren’t completely covered by liquid.
  4. Leave a little liquid in the skillet! The noodles will keep absorbing moisture as the sauce comes together, so the mixture should still look slightly loose once the pasta and broccoli are tender. If the noodles are still firm but most of the broth has disappeared, add a splash of water or broth, cover, and let them cook a little longer. Nobody wants dry chicken and broccoli lasagna!
  5. Be gentle with the cream and cheese. I turn the heat down to low before stirring in the heavy cream, Parmesan, and shredded chicken. Boiling the sauce can make it separate or turn grainy. Also, keep a close eye on the mozzarella under the broiler. It only needs 2-4 minutes to turn bubbly and lightly golden.
  6. Bulk it up with more veggies! I’m all for using what I’ve got, and I think frozen peas, frozen corn, or drained canned corn would be delicious here! Add them with the broccoli so they have time to heat through. For firmer veggies like carrots, dice them small and sauté them with the onion so they have longer to soften. A couple handfuls of fresh or frozen spinach would also be lovely, like I use in my vegetarian stovetop lasagna. Just stir it in near the end until wilted.

Add the seasoned chicken and cook for about 4-5 minutes per side, until lightly golden and the thickest part reaches an internal temperature of 165°F. Transfer the chicken to a plate and set aside. Leave those flavorful browned bits in the skillet.

Sauté the onion and garlic: Reduce the heat to medium and add the diced onion and minced garlic to the same skillet. Cook for 3-4 minutes, stirring frequently, until the onion softens and becomes translucent and the garlic smells fragrant. Stir across the bottom of the skillet to loosen the browned bits left behind by the chicken.

Add the noodles and broth: Pour in 4 cups chicken broth and add 8 oz. broken lasagna noodles (about 9-10 sheets, broken into roughly 2-inch pieces). Stir to distribute the noodles, pressing them down so they’re mostly covered by the broth. Don’t worry if a few pieces stick out!

If the noodles need more cooking time but the liquid is running low, add a splash of water or broth.

Gently mix until everything is coated in a creamy sauce and the chicken is heated through. Avoid letting the mixture boil vigorously, as this can affect the texture of the cream sauce.

Add the mozzarella and broil: Sprinkle 1 cup shredded mozzarella evenly over the skillet. Transfer it to the oven and broil for 2-4 minutes, until the cheese is melted, bubbling, and lightly golden brown in spots. Keep an eye on it so the cheese doesn’t burn!

Storage & Reheating

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3-4 days. Let it cool slightly before transferring it to your container, and refrigerate within 2 hours of cooking. The noodles will absorb the sauce as they sit, so expect leftovers to be a little thicker.

Reheating

I recommend reheating individual portions gently in the microwave or in a skillet over low heat. Add a splash of milk or chicken broth to loosen the sauce and bring back that creamy consistency. Stir occasionally until heated through to 165°F, being careful not to overheat the sauce.

Freezing

This recipe is best enjoyed fresh, but you can freeze leftovers if needed! Let the chicken and broccoli lasagna cool, then transfer it to airtight, freezer-safe containers and freeze for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating gently in the microwave or on the stovetop. I recommend adding a splash of milk or broth to help restore the creaminess, as the sauce may separate slightly after freezing and the noodles will be softer.

This Stuffed Pepper Soup has all the cozy flavors of classic stuffed peppers, with browned, savory ground beef, tender bell peppers, onions, and garlic simmered in a rich, generously seasoned tomato broth. I honestly could not stop eating it! It’s easy, hearty, satisfying, and you get all that familiar flavor without the extra steps of stuffing peppers. I serve it over fluffy rice and, don’t worry, I didn’t forget the cheese! A sprinkle of mozzarella gives you that irresistible cheese pull. Better yet, it’s ready in just 45 minutes and costs only $1.97 per serving. Perfect for soup season, or any season!

Big Flavor from a Simple Shopping List

I already love classic stuffed bell peppers, but this soup makes enjoying those same flavors so much easier on a weeknight. In most stuffed pepper recipes, the rice goes straight into the filling. For this soup, though, I prefer to keep it separate until serving. Rice soaks up liquid like a sponge, and I found that stirring it straight into the pot made the rice mushy. And by the next day, there was barely any broth left! Instead, I like to spoon the rice into individual bowls and ladle the soup over the top. It’s such a simple step, but it makes a big difference…especially when I’m saving leftovers.

I also make the most of my cooking time by getting the rice going while I prepare the soup. Once the ground beef and veggies (bell peppers, onion, and garlic) are cooked, I simmer everything for 15 minutes in a rich tomato broth packed with herbs. And, of course, I wanted to keep this soup easy on the grocery budget, too. I use green bell peppers (which are usually cheaper than red, yellow, or orange), canned tomatoes, dried herbs, and Better Than Bouillon for the broth. Nothing fancy or expensive, but plenty of flavor! These simple ingredients help me stretch just one pound of ground beef into six hearty servings. 😋

Recipe Success Tips

  1. Let those peppers stand out! I cut my bell peppers and onion into a medium dice because I like them to have some texture and really stand out in the finished soup. Keep the pieces roughly the same size so they cook evenly. After the sauté and 15-minute simmer, the peppers should be tender enough to pierce with a fork but still hold their shape. If they’re a little firm, give the soup a few more minutes.
  2. Remove the excess grease. Depending on the type of ground beef you use (I use 27% fat), quite a bit of grease can collect in the pot as it cooks. Make sure to carefully blot up the excess with a paper towel so your soup doesn’t end up oily. You can also spoon the grease into a heatproof container, let it cool, and discard it in the trash. Just don’t pour it down the sink!
  3. Make it as brothy as you like. Prefer a slightly thinner soup? Stir in a splash of extra beef broth or water until you’re happy with the consistency. If there’s more liquid than you’d like, let it simmer uncovered for a few extra minutes, stirring occasionally. I recommend tasting and adjusting the seasoning before serving.
  4. Got extra veggies? Throw them in! This soup is a great way to use up whatever you’ve got. Diced carrots or celery can go in with the peppers and onion, although carrots may need a little longer to soften. Corn can be added with the tomatoes and broth, and fresh spinach only needs a minute or two at the end. You can even add diced potatoes (I’d dice them small, like in our vegetable beef soup), but they’ll need a longer simmer and possibly some extra broth!

Budget-Saving Tip

When I make stuffed peppers at home, I typically use ground beef. However, the ground beef makes up about half the cost of this entire pot of soup, so it’s also a great place to save some money. Feel free to use ground chicken, turkey, pork, or sausage instead. (You can even try lentils!)

Higher-fat ground beef is often cheaper than lean, and it’s totally fine to use here. Just drain off the excess fat after browning so your soup doesn’t end up oily. If you still want some beef in your soup but need to stretch your budget further, try ½ lb. ground beef with 1 cup cooked brown or green lentils. Brown the beef as directed, then stir in the cooked lentils with the tomatoes and broth to heat through during the final simmer. We use a similar meat-stretching trick in our lentil and sausage stew, and it’s a great way to make a little meat go further.

Gather all of your ingredients: Cut 2 green bell peppers into a medium dice, finely dice 1 onion, and mince 2 garlic cloves. I like to get the chopping done first since the rice and soup cook at the same time.

Cook the rice: Add 1 cup uncooked long-grain white rice and 2 cups water to a medium saucepan with a lid. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat to a gentle simmer. Cover and cook for 20 minutes, or until the rice is tender and the water has been absorbed. Turn off the heat and set aside. (This is the same technique we use in our ‘how to cook rice’ guide).

Brown the ground beef: While the rice simmers, heat 1 Tbsp vegetable oil in a large pot over medium heat. Once the oil is hot, add 1 lb. ground beef and cook for about 5 minutes, breaking it into small crumbles as it cooks. The beef should be cooked through, with no pink remaining.

Remove the excess grease: Carefully blot up any excess grease from the beef using paper towels. This helps prevent an oily layer from forming on top of the finished soup.

Soften the vegetables: Add the 2 diced green bell peppers, 1 diced onion, 2 minced garlic cloves, 1½ tsp salt, and 1 tsp black pepper to the pot with the beef. Cook for 5 minutes, stirring occasionally. The onions and peppers should start to soften but still have some texture.

Add the tomatoes and seasonings: Pour in one 14.5 oz. can of diced tomatoes (including the juices), one 15 oz. can of tomato sauce, and 2 cups beef broth. Add 1 tsp dried oregano, ½ tsp dried basil, ¼ tsp dried parsley, and ¼ tsp paprika. Stir everything together until the seasonings are evenly distributed throughout the tomato broth.

Simmer the soup: Bring the mixture to a gentle simmer and cook for 15 minutes to allow the flavors to develop and the broth to thicken slightly. The bell peppers should be tender enough to pierce easily with a fork while still holding their shape. If they’re still too firm, let the soup simmer a little longer.

Serve with rice and cheese: Fluff the cooked rice with a fork and divide it evenly among six serving bowls (about ½ cup per bowl).

Ladle a heaping cup of stuffed pepper soup over each portion of rice, then sprinkle with the shredded mozzarella (1 cup total). Serve warm and enjoy! (For a bonus, I sprinkled fresh parsley on top. Storage & Reheating

I recommend storing any leftover rice and soup separately because the rice will continue absorbing the broth as it sits. Let everything cool promptly, then transfer to separate airtight containers and refrigerate for up to 3-4 days.

Reheating

Reheat the soup in a saucepan over medium heat, stirring occasionally, or in the microwave until heated through. Warm the rice separately in the microwave (with a splash of water, stirring every 15-30 seconds until hot), then spoon the soup over the top. If you’ve already mixed the rice into the soup and it’s become too thick, add a splash of beef broth or water to thin it out as it reheats.

Freezing

You can freeze leftover stuffed pepper soup for up to 3 months in freezer-safe containers. I recommend freezing the rice separately to keep it from getting mushy. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator, or use the thaw setting on your microwave, then reheat thoroughly before serving.
