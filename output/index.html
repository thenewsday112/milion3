Brownie Batter Bark

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1-2/3 cups almond flour
  • 1/3 cup baking cocoa
  • 3 tablespoons packed brown sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup coconut oil, melted and slightly cooled
  • 1-1/2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 1-1/2 cups miniature semisweet chocolate chips, divided
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. Line a 15x10x1-in. baking sheet with parchment paper.
  2. In a large bowl, combine almond flour, cocoa powder, brown sugar and salt. Stir in melted coconut oil and vanilla until smooth. Spread onto the prepared baking sheet in an even layer. Freeze 20 minutes.
  3. Meanwhile, in a small microwave-safe bowl, microwave 1 cup chocolate chips in 30-second increments until melted, stirring in between. Spread over the frozen bark. Sprinkle with the remaining 1/2 cup chocolate chips.
  4. Freeze until firm, about 1 hour. Break into bark pieces.

Nutrition Facts

1 serving: 588 calories, 45g fat (25g saturated fat), 0 cholesterol, 203mg sodium, 44g carbohydrate (33g sugars, 8g fiber), 9g protein.

Azalea Cocktail

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • Ice cubes
  • 4 ounces lemonade
  • 1-1/2 ounces vodka
  • 1/2 ounce grenadine syrup
  • Orange slice and cocktail cherry, for garnish
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. Fill a chilled rocks glass with ice.
  2. Add ice cubes, lemonade, vodka and grenadine to a cocktail shaker. Shake until chilled, 15-20 seconds. Strain into the prepared glass.
  3. Garnish with an orange slice and cocktail cherry to serve.

Nutrition Facts

1 cocktail: 196 calories, 0 fat (0 saturated fat), 0 cholesterol, 8mg sodium, 25g carbohydrate (25g sugars, 0 fiber), 0 protein.

Quick bites from around the food safety arena

  • Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. announced the first update of infant formula nutrient requirements in 30 years. However, the announcement did not identify any new standards for infant formula, nor did it address dozens of cases of cronobacter and botulism that have affected babies since 2021. Although the FDA is accepting public comments on a new proposed rule, it has not yet made that rule available to the public.
  • Thirteen cases of Shiga toxin-producing E. coli may be linked to exposure at a pumpkin patch in California’s San Luis Obispo County. Brookshire Farms has closed while public health officials complete an investigation of the source of the infections. Meanwhile, the petting zoo at another pumpkin farm in Homer Glen, IL, is closed after five children were hospitalized with E. coli infections. All five had visited the farm before becoming ill.
  • The FDA has declared an outbreak of E coli associated with chicken wraps is over. Although the agency did not name Trader Joe’s as the source of the wraps, the USDA’s Food Safety and Inspection Service did confirm that connection to USA Today. The FDA has not completed its investigation as it continues to trace ingredients and inspect sites potentially involved in the outbreak.
  • A second Italian region has barred the serving of uncooked tomatoes after reports of Salmonella infections. Authorities in Emilia-Romagna ordered all public and private food service providers in schools, care facilities, hospitals and nursing homes to avoid the use of uncooked cherry tomatoes. A national task force has been convened to investigate multiple Salmonella outbreaks.
  • A researcher at Cornell University is developing a computerized model aimed at standardizing the way food safety risks are described and assessed during the “last mile” of the supply chain. Cornell University professor and principal investigator Martin Wiedmann built a QMRA, or quantitative microbial risk assessment, tool to improve identification of food safety risks. The tool puts physical damage or defects into the assessment along with other food safety risks. One goal of the research, Wiedmann says, is to get people “more committed to doing more standardized quality assessments.”

Today’s Topic: Hunting Season

Like others that have gone before it, this year’s hunting season is about not one, but many stories: changing demographics, economic impacts, a unique food safety culture and a little politics. 

Spend some time at any destination retail “fashion to fishing” store, like Scheels, Bass Pro Shops, or Cabela’s, and you'll see it for yourself.

Women are fueling a resurgence in fishing and hunting. It may not reach the peaks of 50 years ago, but the significance cannot be understated.

Scheels and Bass Pro Shops, which acquired Cabela's Inc. for $4.5 billion in 2016, represent the retail clout of hunting season. Before and during hunting season, couples exiting one of these stores with “his and her” shotguns are a common sight.

There are about 15.2 million state-licensed hunters in the U.S., and twice as many are licensed to fish. They’re all busy now; it’s hunting season.
