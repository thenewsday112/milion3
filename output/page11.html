Macro Bowls: The Inspiration

Mastering The Art of Plant-Based Cooking is a 484 page beast of a book. Joe Yonan has authored a wonderfully comprehensive (globally inspired) cookbook and included an all-star list of contributors. If you’re the kind of person who owns just a dozen cookbooks and are plant-curious related to cooking, this book should be one of them. What is a macro bowl?

Macro bowls are the ultimate feel-good food. They check the boxes next to important macronutrients like carbs, fat, fiber, and protein in a single bowl. They’re also endlessly adaptable, making them a great go-to all year.

Details: Brown Rice

The foundation of these macro bowls is good, well-cooked brown rice. I like the boil and drain method of cooking the recipe guides you through. It works brilliantly every time. So, if cooking rice makes you nervous, give it a try. For these bowls, a few of my favorite brown rices are: Koda Farms Organic Whole Grain Brown Kokuho Rose, Lundberg Organic Short Grain Brown Rice, and Massa Organics Medium-Grain Brown Rice.

Macro Bowls: Variations

Use what’s in season – I used in-season vegetables from the farmers’ market for the bowls you see pictured here – broccoli, delicata squash, and yellow beans. For reference, it’s December here in California. Other bowls recently have included cauliflower florets, small cabbage wedges, beets, and ribbons of carrot. When we make our way into spring, asparagus, fava beans, will be in the mix. And you can expand beyond steaming as a cooking method if you like. During colder winter months I sometimes integrate roasted ingredients (375°F tossed with a bit of olive oil), or do a combination of roasted and steamed. For this bowl I had roasted delicata on hand.

The Recipe:

The recipe below reflects all the tweaks and personal preferences I’ve come to make.  I always make double dressing, sometimes more. This makes for easy leftover meals and drizzles. I also tend to use tofu and do a quick pan-fry to get it golden and hot while my steamer is heating up, and sprinkle the tofu generously with five spice when done cooking – this is the one I tend to keep on hand. And, per my comments above, I grab for whatever veggies I have on hand, a rotating cast. The nature of macro bowls is flexibility, so have fun with them.

Chicken with Cajun Sauce

5 Stars 4 Stars 3 Stars 2 Stars 1 Star

5 from 15 reviews

  • Author: Lindsay
  • Total Time: 40 minutes
  • Yield: 4 servings 1x
Print Pin

Description

Pan-fried chicken in a luscious Cajun cream sauce, served with mashed potatoes and green beans – loving this easy, cozy weeknight dinner!

Ingredients

Units USM

Chicken:

  • 1 tablespoon avocado oil
  • 1 lb. chicken breast
  • 1 1/2 teaspoon Cajun seasoning (I used McCormick)

Sauce:

  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 1 shallot, minced or thinly sliced
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
  • 1 teaspoon Cajun seasoning (I used McCormick)
  • 1 tablespoon flour
  • 3/4 cup heavy cream
  • 1 cup chicken broth (I use water + 1 tsp Better Than Bouillon)

For serving: 

  • Mashed Potatoes
  • Green beans or salad
Cook Mode Prevent your screen from going dark

Instructions

  1. Prep the Chicken: Cut the chicken into thinner pieces horizontally, or pound them out a bit so they’re thinner.
  2. Cook the Chicken: Season the chicken filets with Cajun seasoning. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat (I use a nonstick for this). Sauté the chicken on each side for 3-4 minutes, until the internal temperature reaches 165 and the outside is golden; remove from the pan and set aside on a plate.
  3. Sauté Shallots: Wipe off any overly browned bits from the pan. Turn the heat to medium-low so the butter and shallots don’t burn. Add butter and shallots; saute until softened and fragrant.
  4. Make The Roux: Add tomato paste and Cajun seasoning; saute for 3-4 minutes until the tomato paste has developed a rich color. Add flour and mix; cook for 2-3 minutes to remove any floury taste.
  5. Finish Sauce: Add broth gradually; whisk to incorporate after each addition. Once it’s smooth, add heavy cream and whisk again to incorporate. Let it simmer for 5-10 minutes to thicken slightly. Add chicken back to the pan so it is partially submerged with sauce.
  6. Serve: Serve pieces of chicken with a spoonful of the sauce over the top. I love this SO MUCH with mashed potatoes and broccoli – see notes!

Notes

To air fry the chicken: Air fry at 375 for 10-12 minutes, or until at least 165 degrees internal temperature.

Roasted Potatoes and Broccoli (Oven): Toss 3-4 cups halved baby potatoes with avocado oil and salt on a sheet pan. Roast the potatoes at 425 for 15 minutes. Scoot the potatoes over, add 3-4 cups broccoli and ¼ cup water to the same sheet pan, and roast for another 15 minutes.

Roasted Potatoes and Broccoli (Air Fryer): Toss 2 cups halved baby potatoes with avocado oil and salt. Air fry at 375 for 12 minutes. Add 2 cups frozen or fresh broccoli; air fry at 400 for another 10-12 minutes.

Moelleux of Summer Fruits

📅 Sep 12, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

This may or may not happen to you, but sometimes when I’m a guest in someone’s home, where everyone pitches in to make dinner, all eyes (or just the host’s eyes) fall on me to make dessert. Gulp. I happy to pitch in with the cooking or bringing along a few baked goodies for the weekend, but I’m not always prepared to bake on the spot.

If you’re lucky, there may be a scale, a cake pan, a sharp knife, and hopefully sugar, butter, eggs, and flour. If you’re really lucky, there might be a whisk and a spatula, too. But if you’ve ever tried to peel a dozen apples with a steak knife because it’s the only one that’s even close to sharp, or eyeball 1 cup/140g) of flour, you know what I’m talking about. I once did a post on Things I Bring When I’m a Guest for the Weekend, which raised the ire of some, but I require a minimum of a knife, a spatula, a bowl, and a cake pan if I’m going to bake something, like a cake.

You’re likely familiar with the Moelleux au chocolat, the soft warm individual melting cakes that everybody loves. (If you have the kind of friends that have six to eight ramekins, well, that’s great.) I recently saw French food writer and journalist François-Régis Gaudy (his book, Let’s Eat France, translated into English, is a lot of fun and pretty amazing) make a Moelleux à la abricot on his Instagram page, which didn’t require a mixer or any special equipment. Not even a whisk!

His Apricot Cake was a very casual affair, baked in a rectangular mold on a sheet of parchment paper, and came out of the oven rather free-form, looking exactly like something that would come out of a French home cook’s kitchen, when baking dessert for friends. I’m fine with that but didn’t know if you were, so I thought I’d tinker with it and make it in a cake pan, which is easier to slice. I also fiddled around with the ingredients, as well as putting the brown granulated sugar on top (rather than underneath), to give the moelleux a nice sheen.

I used a springform cake pan but you can use any similar-sized vessel; an 8-, 9- or 10-square or round cake pan would work, or even a baking dish. The baking time will vary but if your experience with using a variety of ovens in other people’s homes is anything like mine, you’ll find that each one is different, and what one says is 350ºF/180ºC will be different than another whose dial says 350ºF/180ºC. I don’t travel with an oven thermometer but may add that to things I bring when I’m staying with people.

Tip: If you’re staying in someone’s place for the weekend, you can mix the dry ingredients in a container or zip-top bag then put the dessert together when you get there.

I made one cake with apricots first, which was sensational, but knowing fresh apricots are harder to come by elsewhere, I made another one with plums, which came out great as well. It would work with other summer fruits and berries but I really like the tanginess of apricots or plums against the moist cake layer, so stuck with those two. A handful of berries wouldn’t hurt either, but I do recommend keeping it simple.

Moelleux aux fruits d'ete

Inspired by a recipe from French food writer and journalist François-Régis Gaudry The ground almonds are really delicious in the dessert, but if you can't get ground almonds (sometimes called almond meal or almond flour), you can use 1 1/4 cups (175g) of all-purpose flour in place of the mix. The picture in the post of the batter shows 3 eggs being added, and I took that photo when I first tested the recipe, which I did a few more times after that. The final recipe that I came up with, below, uses 2 eggs. Course Dessert Servings 8 servings

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds (680g) apricots or plums (or another summer fruit, see headnote)
  • 3/4 cup, plus 2 tablespoons (100g) almond flour
  • 1/2 cup (65g) all-purpose flour
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder preferably aluminum-free
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup plus 2 tablespoons (230g) granulated sugar
  • 5 ounces (140g) unsalted butter at room temperature
  • 2 large eggs at room temperature
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract (or 1/2 teaspoon almond extract)
  • 1 1/2 tablespoons coarse granulated brown sugar such as tubinado sugar (or granulated white sugar)

Instructions

  • Halve the fruit and remove the pits. Slice the fruit into 3/4- to 1-inch (2-3cm) wedges. Set aside. Preheat the oven to 350ºF (180ºC.) Grease a 9-inch (23cm) springform pan very well with butter or non-stick spray. (If you don't have a springform pan, you can use another cake pan but it may be harder to get the cake out so you may want to also line the bottom with parchment paper.)
  • In a medium mixing bowl, stir together the almond flour, all-purpose flour, baking powder, salt, and sugar. (My preferred tool to use for this step is a silicone spatula.) Add the soft butter and mix it in with the spatula until it's broken up into little pieces, roughly the size of kernels of corn. Stir in the eggs and vanilla extract, until it's almost smooth. It's fine if there are small pieces of butter visible.
  • Scrape the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top. Arrange the wedges of fruit on their sides in concentric circles over the batter, snugly placing the wedges against each other, pressing them gently into the batter as you go. Avoid putting them right up against the sides of the pan. It's best to leave some room for the batter to rise between the fruit and the pan, to avoid juices from the fruit adhering to the sides of the pan as the moelleux bakes. (It's unavoidable - and natural - if using juicy fruits, like plums, to have juice run out. But placing the slices not right up against the sides of the pan will help you get the cake out of the pan later.)
  • Sprinkle the coarse sugar over the top and bake until the center just feels set; a toothpick inserted into the center should come out free of cake crumbs stuck to it, about 1 hour. Remove from the oven and set on a cooling rack. If any fruit juices have bubbled up and stuck to the sides of the pan, run a knife around the outside of the cake, which will help it release later.

Notes

Notes: If you want to make this gluten-free, I'd go with the almond flour rendition, and use a gluten-free flour substitute in place of the wheat flour. If you want to reduce the sugar in the recipe, you could cut it by 6 tablespoons/75g and skip the sugar on top, although I wouldn't.

Chocolate Olive Oil Spread

A delicious dark chocolate spread with olive oil that you're whole family will enjoy. The recipe is adapted from my book, The Great Book of Chocolate – now available! Course Breakfast, Snack Keyword chocolate, olive oil, spread Servings 8

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup (70g) powdered sugar
  • 1/4 cup (25g) unsweetened cocoa powder preferably Dutch process
  • 4 ounces (115g) bittersweet or semisweet chocolate chopped
  • 1/2 cup (125ml) olive oil
  • Flaky sea salt such as Maldon or fleur de sel

Instructions

  • Sift together the powdered sugar and cocoa powder in a small bowl.
  • Warm the chopped chocolate and olive oil over low heat in a medium saucepan, stirring until the chocolate is melted and smooth. With a whisk, stir in the powdered sugar and cocoa powder mixture.
  • Transfer the mixture to small bowl and refrigerate until it's spreadable, which can take 2 to 3 hours. Check it every 15 minutes until it's the consistency of chocolate pudding. Scrape the mixture into a jar and store at room temperature until ready to serve.

Notes

The spread can be stored at room temperature for up to two weeks. It can be refrigerated for up to one month. Let it come to room temperature before serving.  For more information about cocoa powder, check out my Cocoa powder FAQs.

Affogato

A delicious dessert with ice cream drowned in coffee liqueur, rum, and amaro Course Drinks Cuisine Italian Keyword affogato, amaro, coffee liqueur, ice cream, rum Servings 2

Ingredients

  • 3/4 ounce (20ml) dark rum
  • 3/4 ounce (20ml) amaro
  • 3/4 ounce (20ml) coffee liqueur
  • Ice cream or sorbet
  • 2 shots espresso (cooled slightly)

Instructions

  • In a small pitcher or measuring cup, mix together the rum, amaro, and coffee liqueur.
  • Put one generous scoop, or several smaller scoops, of ice cream or sorbet into two ice cream dishes or small bowls. Douse each serving with the mix of liqueurs.
  • Add a shot of espresso to each portion and serve.

Notes

You can use your favorite brands of liqueur here. In this recipe, Margot used Rabarbaro Nardini amaro, but others, such as Fernet-Branca or Ramazzotti, would work. Nowadays there are non-alcoholic versions of most liqueurs, including the ones used here. If you’re avoiding alcohol, you can use non-alcoholic alternatives.  For serving, feel free to shave some dark chocolate over the top of each affogato.

This savory tart is something I threw together with all the odds and ends of a recent farmers’ market haul – herbs, scallions, a few banana peppers, zucchini blossoms, and some goat cheese. All that yellow – that’s me hanging on to summer! I keep pie dough in the freezer, especially this time of year, for impromptu tarts and pies, and because of that, this tart came together in no time. Why This Tart is So Simple

This isn’t a custard tart. It’s simply a market tart, where ingredients are piled onto a rolled-out piece of dough and baked once until deeply golden. You don’t have to par-bake the crust, and you don’t have to worry about the filling setting before the crust gets too dark. The process is a lot like making homemade pizza. Add your favorite toppings and bake. I like to eat a wedge of tart like this one alongside a bright, fresh, leafy salad.

Banana Pepper & Goat Cheese Tart: Ingredients

  • Banana Peppers: I jump on banana peppers whenever I see them, but they can be tricky to find. They have a little bite and add a fantastic zip to whatever you’re making – tart, pizza, salad, etc. Swap in another pepper you enjoy if banana peppers are elusive. I picked up some gorgeous, mild Cornito Giallo peppers the other day as well – or  even a yellow bell pepper would work! Can you use the pickled banana peppers in jars? Sure! They’ll be tangier than fresh peppers, but equally fun to experiment with on tarts like this one.
  • Zucchini Blossoms: Grab a few if you see them. They’re so special and beautiful, and I cut them into ribbon-like flecks here. If you can’t find them, just add another herb you like, or another edible flower – nasturtium or basil flowers would be nice!
  • Herbs: I opted for chives and basil, but if you can imagine another herb working well with goat cheese, by all means, go for it.
  • Goat cheese: I like using creamy, spreadable cheeses for tarts like this one. Labneh is another favorite in this sort of tart.

Shaping the Tart

The whole goal with a tart like this one is minimal fuss. And this includes shaping. You can bake it in a plan if you like but more often a free-form shape is in order. You can fold over the edges of your dough into a galette shape, or do what I’ve done here – fold the edge of the pie dough onto itself and then do a deep crimp using the thumb and pointer finger on one hand and the pointer finger on the other – see photo above.

I skipped the freezer step on this one (above), and you can see the issue. My crimping lost its definition, but aside from that it was really tasty and hit the lunchtime mark.

Soft Tofu with Pork and Shrimp Gravy (Taohu Song Krueng)

This is the Thai version of mapo tofu! Soft tofu in a sauce made with ground pork or chicken, shrimp, and any kind of vegetables you like. A great fridge cleanout recipe that's also kid friendly! Course Main Course Cuisine Thai Prep Time 20 minutes minutes Cook Time 15 minutes minutes Servings 3 servings

Ingredients

Sauce

  • 1 tablespoon oyster sauce or sub GF oyster sauce
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce or sub GF soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon Chinese cooking wine or sake
  • 1 teaspoon granulated sugar
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 2 teaspoons tapioca starch or cornstarch

The Rest

  • 2-3 Tablespoons neutral oil
  • 1 tube egg tofu or soft soy tofu (about 250 g per tube) cut in ¾-inch thick pieces (see note 1)
  • 4 cloves garlic chopped
  • ½ cup diced carrots
  • ½ cup diced onion
  • 1 teaspoon fish sauce
  • 8 oz ground pork or ground chicken
  • 1 cup diced crunchy veg of your choice see note 2
  • 8 oz tomatoes small diced
  • White pepper to taste
  • 150 g shrimp (optional) chopped into small pieces, or use more ground pork
  • Chopped cilantro or green onions for garnish
  • Jasmine rice for serving

Instructions

  • Make the sauce by combining the oyster sauce, soy sauce, Chinese cooking wine, sugar and water in a small bowl and stir until the sugar is dissolved. In another small bowl, dissolve the tapioca starch in a splash of water (a couple of tablespoons or so). Set aside. 1 tablespoon oyster sauce, 1 tablespoon soy sauce, 1 tablespoon Chinese cooking wine, 1 teaspoon granulated sugar, 2 tablespoons water, 2 teaspoons tapioca starch
  • Optional step: Pan fry the tofu. In a nonstick pan or wok, add enough oil to thoroughly cover the bottom and heat until hot over medium high heat. Add the tofu pieces and sear until golden on each side, 3 to 4 minutes per side. Remove from the pan and place them in a shallow serving bowl. 2-3 Tablespoons neutral oil, 1 tube egg tofu or soft soy tofu (about 250 g per tube)
  • In the same pan, pour our excess oil if necessary so you have just a little bit to coat the bottom. On medium high heat, add the garlic, onions, carrots and the fish sauce and saute for a few minutes until the onions are translucent. 4 cloves garlic, ½ cup diced carrots, ½ cup diced onion, 1 tsp fish sauce
  • Add the ground pork and stir to break it up into small pieces, then add all the remaining vegetables, the tomatoes, the soy sauce mixture (not the starch slurry) and the white pepper. Cook, stirring frequently, until the tomatoes are mushy and have released most of their juices, and the vegetables are done to your liking. 8 oz ground pork, 1 cup diced crunchy veg of your choice, 8 oz tomatoes, White pepper
  • Stir the tapioca starch slurry as it will have settled, then pour about half of it into the pan while stirring. The sauce should thicken up immediately and reaches full thickening power once the sauce returns to a boil. You can add the other half of the slurry if you feel it needs more thickening.
  • Add the chopped shrimp (if using), and cook just until the shrimp is done. If it looks like it needs more liquid, add a splash of water. If you opted not to fry the tofu, put the tofu into the sauce now and let it simmer in the sauce to heat up for a couple of minutes. 150 g shrimp
  • Turn off the heat and do a final seasoning check, adding more soy sauce or fish sauce (in my case I added Golden Mountain Sauce) as desired.
  • Plate. If you fried the tofu, ladle the sauce over the fried tofu. Garnish with cilantro or green onions if you wish and serve with jasmine rice. Chopped cilantro or green onions, Jasmine rice

  2. For crunchy veg, I used celery and kohlrabi, but other options are green beans, broccoli (especially the stems), cauliflower, or baby corn.

Multiple times a year I look around and declare that this is my favorite time of year to cook.  I love it in the winter when citrus season peaks. I love it when fava beans are the brightest green with the silkiest, creamiest texture (before they go too starchy). I wrote everything down in a list —lemongrass, banana peppers, fresh cranberry beans, baby romaine, etc.. — and then started daydreaming about what I might make. The first thing was this summer-maxxing lunch salad. High Salad Lunch Salad: Ingredients

The key here is assembling peak everything. If the tomatoes where you are look a little sad, skip them and bump up one of the other ingredients. Or, swap in another ingredient you love – this salad is very forgiving.

  • Tomatoes: My neighbor Emily tipped me off to just how good the Green Zebra tomatoes were this week, and she was spot on. I love their concentrated flavor and beautiful lime green and yellow striped markings. Keep an eye out for heirloom tomatoes where you are, and go for the ones with the best flavor.
  • Pluots: I love the complexity of a perfect, ripe pluot, and while a lot of people I know wait for peach season, I’m actually team pluot. You can use either here.
  • Crispy Shallots: A lot of people buy these, but I like to make my own. Fry thinly sliced shallots in a good amount of oil until deeply browned, then use a spider to transfer them to a paper towel. It’s one of the assertive elements that also brings crunch and savoriness to this salad.
  • Banana Peppers: Keep an eye out! I love their color and mild bite and spiciness. If you can’t find them and like bell peppers, swap in some yellow or red bell peppers. Quite different, but reasonable!
  • Vinegar: The vinegar here is important and should taste delicious. I’ve been using a red wine vinegar that is agrodolce – a bit sweet, but still plenty acidic (it’s this one). If you don’t have an agrodolce vinegar, consider adding a bit of honey or sugar to your vinaigrette to round it out a bit. Just enough to get it to your liking. 

A few of the other ingredients and flowers that were at the market at the same time. On the ingredient front there were: perfect carrots, yellow beans, fresh cranberry beans, shallots, lemongrass, and some little lettuces hiding in there. Marigolds made an appearance after weeks and weeks of show-stopping dahlias. It’s one of the first things I notice as we round the corner into fall. 

High Summer Lunch Salad: Serving Ideas

I served this salad basically as a meal on its own, with a bit of Zanzibari Sesame Flatbread from The Food of Oman (page 177), published in 2015. Sidenote: this is a wonderful book that Ken at Now Serving turned me on to a few years back. I also added a little plate of sheep’s milk feta drizzled with olive oil, more of a creamy bonus than an essential.
